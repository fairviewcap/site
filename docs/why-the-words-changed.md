# Strategic Messaging Migration & Content Audit
**Client:** Fairview Capital  
**Scope:** Brand Position, Copywriting Evolution, Compliance Defense, and Information Architecture  
**Core Premise:** *Facts did not change. Voice did.*

---

## Executive Summary

This document details the transition from the legacy [fairviewcap.com](https://www.fairviewcap.com) copy to the updated digital platform.

In high-net-worth wealth management, generic industry terms—*fiduciary, fee-only, holistic, personalized, boutique, long-term*—no longer differentiate a firm; they merely register it in the category. The updated language does not invent a new position for Fairview. Instead, it translates Fairview’s existing 30-year operational history into clear, high-contrast, and legally defensible statements that immediately distinguish the firm from wirehouses, PE-backed aggregators, and model-based RIAs.

> **The Core Thesis:**  
> If a sentence could sit on a competitor’s website unchanged, it does not belong on Fairview's. Competitors can buy adjectives; they cannot buy 1995, an in-house research book, or a founder who turned down private equity buyouts to remain 100% advisor-owned.

Quotes are from the live WordPress site as of 19 Aug 2026 (home, Our Firm, Difference, founder’s note, Pricing, Wealth, the investment-research-process pages, Team). Old wealth and investment were three pages each; `/investment-management` 404s. This is not a CCO sign-off. The numbers stay aligned with what the firm already publishes. Counsel should still read the site. The rewrite is *how those facts are said*.

## 1. The Strategic Argument

### The Category Landscape

Modern high-net-worth families evaluating advisors typically open three to four browser tabs. Every firm in the category makes identical claims, making true differentiation difficult to parse on the surface:

| Firm Category | Typical Operational Reality | Typical Marketing Language |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wirehouse / Bank** | Product sales, sales quotas, corporate parent to feed | *“Holistic advice. Your goals come first.”* |
| **PE Aggregator** | Acquired firm, rebranded, high advisor turnover risk | *“Independent. Local. Fiduciary commitment.”* |
| **Robo / Model Shop** | Automated portfolios, third-party model funds, no human judgment | *“Sophisticated. Personalized. Low cost.”* |
| **Typical Indie RIA** | Outsources stock research; uses standard industry template copy | *“Boutique. In-house expertise. Best interests.”* |

Fairview is in this category. The site does not invent a difference. It makes the real one visible in the first screen — so a family comparing tabs can tell which shop this is.

### What High-Net-Worth Prospects Are Actually Testing

Before filling out a contact form, a serious prospect is evaluating four core structural questions:

1. **Ownership:** Who owns the firm, and who receives the profits?
2. **Research:** Who actually picks the stocks and analyzes the balance sheets?
3. **Cost:** What is the exact, unhidden price of the relationship?
4. **Longevity:** Will the people managing my money today still be here in 15 years?

They are also testing competence and character — will you grow and manage the money well, and are you their kind of people. Character is how they decide to trust the work. It is not a substitute for it.

These were already true. The old site listed the work as capabilities and the people as adjectives. The new site uses both as the position.

### How Fairview Demonstrates Truth Over Claims

| What Most RIAs Claim | What Fairview Demonstrates |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fiduciary** *(Required by law)* | **One Boss:** Employee-owned; PE buyouts actively turned down. |
| **“In-House Research”** | **Direct Ownership:** A research team that names all 25–30 companies in the book. |
| **Growth & Scale** | **Organic Referrals:** Zero salespeople; zero cold calls; growth driven by client referrals. |
| **“Transparent Fees”** | **Public Schedule:** Full fee schedule and interactive estimator on the page, labeled illustrative. |
| **Personalized Service** | **Specific Work:** Direct planning, custom tax management, coordination with outside CPAs/attorneys. |
| **“You’ll like us”** | **A room you would trust:** The letter, no salesperson, a polite no, Saturday goalie. |
| **Long-Term Partner** | **Proven History:** Founded in 1995; 17 team members; partner equity buy-in. |
| **For affluent families in [metro]** | **A clear no:** For families who want *this* shop — not every investor. |

**The position, in one line:** Fairview is the independent, advisor-owned firm that still does its own research and still picks up the phone — for families who want their money managed the long way, and do not need a product. That is the test for every page, not a slogan.

### Brand Culture & Voice Guidelines

- **Culture:** The copy reflects a real operational shop—17 people, a short client list, in-house research, and zero sales personnel. It reflects an advisor-owned firm where equity was passed to long-time team members rather than cashed out to an outside buyer. Andy and Marcia still run it. The site has to sound like that room, or the position is a poster.
- **Voice:** Modeled on the plainspoken, unhurried, and intellectually honest tone of Andy Mathieson talking across a kitchen table. It avoids closer's urgency, buzzwords, and marketing fluff in favor of clarity and character. Intelligence is assumed; character is the point. This is Andy’s voice: modest, moral, specific, and unwilling to pretend. It is not written in anyone else’s.

| Operating principle | What it means on the page |
| :--- | :--- |
| Character before close | No salesperson. The job is to be worth a referral. |
| Enough families to know them | The book is limited on purpose. |
| The work is done here | Someone in the building reads the business. Slow is allowed. |
| Say the no | If you are a poor fit, you say so. |
| Owners, not a product | The partner buy-in made that visible. Andy did not extract. |
| Neighbors, not a program | Coaching, puppies, Saturday goalie — not a CSR pillar. |

A family buying a thirty-year relationship is buying this culture, not Andy’s calendar. The copy is a transcript of the shop, not a tribute to him.

| The industry default | What the new copy does instead |
| :--- | :--- |
| Stacked adjectives (*expert, ethical, effective*) | One true sentence |
| “Holistic / personalized / boutique” | Named work: plan, portfolio, the other professionals |
| “We believe…” | A number, a refusal, or a picture |
| Superlatives (*outstanding, exceptional, best possible*) | As-of dates, Form ADV, “illustrative, not a quote” |
| A closer’s urgency | A filter: *we aren’t for everyone* |
| Certainty about markets | “Most economists are more often wrong than right” |
| “Let’s start a conversation” | “Let’s talk” / “if we can’t help, we’ll say so” |

The old site was not dishonest. It was generic. Andy is not. That is faster honesty, not louder marketing. It also filters — predictions, exotic products, a shop that might belong to someone else in five years. That protects the book.

## 2. Compliance & Legal Rationale

This industry does not forgive sloppy adjectives. **Counsel still reviews.** The new site is written so that review starts ahead.

The Marketing Rule cares about unsubstantiated claims, superlatives, implied results, and performance. Brochure English is full of those.

| Retired language | Why it came out |
| :--- | :--- |
| *Expert, Ethical, Effective* | Alliteration is not a standard. “Ethical” is a life, not a headline. |
| *the highest level of…* / *strives to provide* | Superlative. Highest compared to whom? Striving is weaker than doing. |
| *exceptional client retention* | A results claim with no number, no period, no backup. |
| *outstanding advice* / *best possible experience* | Self-grade. A lawyer hedges it; a family skips it. |
| *optimize client outcomes* | Sounds like a performance promise. |
| *holistic advice* / *constituencies* / *We believe that…* | Category words. Plan, one boss, a number, or a refusal instead. |
| SEO titles about *affluent families in the San Francisco Bay Area* | Marketing inventory. Can look like a testimonial you did not collect. |

**Plain speech is tighter compliance.** The rewrite stopped saying things you cannot put a binder under. The facts below were already true. They are no longer buried.

**What the new site does on purpose**

- **Sourced claims.** 1995, Marin. AUM **$2.14B** as of Q1 2026. 100% employee-owned. Typical $2M. Fee tiers 1.00 / 0.75 / 0.50. One AUM fee, no commissions, planning included. RIA and fiduciary next to the schedule. ADV and CRS in the footer. Same jurisdictional disclaimer.
- **No performance ads.** No returns table, no “we beat the market,” no cherry-picked chart. The investment page states an *objective*. If counsel wants it softer, soften the verb, not the honesty.
- **Fee tool labeled.** “Illustrative only — not a quote or offer.” Family aggregation disclosed. ADV governs.
- **Same jurisdictional footer** as the old site, in substance.
- **No usual traps.** No fake urgency, no “guaranteed,” no invented testimonials, no celebrity comparison.
- **“Not for everyone”** reduces implied suitability.
- **Learn is the work**, not search bait.

**Facts counsel should still tick** (true as the firm has told them; if one is off, change the fact, not put the adjectives back):

- Never hired a salesperson / never made a cold call / 0 salespeople
- Turned down PE and aggregators; partner buy-in rather than an outside sale
- “We can name what we own”
- Seventeen people
- Outside assets advised at no extra charge
- Book described as 25–30 names (old public text said 15–25 — confirm)

Counsel is being asked to bless fewer claims, dated numbers, a labeled estimator, the same disclaimer, and a site that tells some people to leave — not a louder campaign.

## 3. Page-by-Page Copy Comparison

### Home — `/`

**Original**

> Expert, Ethical, Effective Wealth Management
>
> Fairview Capital strives to provide the highest level of personalized wealth management services and professional investment advisory expertise. Our holistic advice, disciplined investment process, and business decisions are guided by our clients’ best interests and unique needs. We serve all of our constituencies—clients, employees and community—with integrity and fairness.

Then a firm overview (1995, more than $2 billion as of 31 Dec 2025, exceptional retention) and a nine-point “Fairview Difference” list. A lot of work, in the same dialect as every peer.

**New**

> Most firms answer to a board, a bank, or a buyer. Ours answers to the phone ringing.
>
> When a firm serves outside owners, they have two mouths to feed—shareholders, then you. We built Fairview so we only have one boss. You.

Proof row: **1995 / 100% employee-owned / $2.14B.** Then real next steps, not a second brochure.

It used to *describe* independence. It now *demonstrates* it. A family comparing tabs can decide in ten seconds whether this is their kind of shop. “Highest level” and “exceptional retention” came out. AUM stayed, with a date.

### Why Fairview — `/firm/why-fairview`

Was `/our-firm`, `/our-firm/the-fairview-difference`, and the founder’s note.

**Original difference page.** Headline: *We Are Dedicated to Providing Outstanding Advice and Service.* Nine capabilities (boutique, tax efficient, personalized…). The don’t-list was already how the firm thinks — lost in the shuffle, one-size-fits-all, outside shareholders, hidden fees — filed after a compliment to the self.

**Original firm page.** 1995; more than $2 billion; exceptional retention; generally $2 million; California and Pennsylvania; independent RIA; fiduciary; 100% employee-owned. All still true. Said in the order a firm talks about itself, not the order a family worries.

**New page** is a plot, not a checklist: tenure and refusal → Andy’s letter → one fee → we can name what we own → the numbers (1995 / $2.14B / 100%, then $2M / 17 / 0 salespeople) → crises we lived → *we aren’t the right firm for everyone.*

A list of “personalized advice” does not differentiate. “0 salespeople” and “we turned those offers down” do. The closer is a filter, not a pitch.

**Founder’s letter — original**

> In March 1995, with young children, a big mortgage, and a belief in a better way, I started Fairview Capital. My mission was to build trusting lifelong relationships with clients by providing highly informed investment decisions and prudent wealth management. … we seek to maximize returns while minimizing expenses. … an unrelenting focus on optimizing client outcomes.

The opening is Andy. The middle slides into mission-statement English.

**New letter** keeps the opening, then:

> The premise was simple: do our own deep research, give honest advice, and put clients first.
>
> Over thirty-one years, as Fairview grew, the offers came—private equity firms and aggregators wanting to buy us out. We turned those offers down. And just last year, rather than cashing out to an outside buyer, we helped our long-time team members become partners and buy equity in the firm.
>
> Fairview is 100% independent and advisor-owned — and we intend to keep it that way.

“Maximize returns while minimizing expenses” came out — true, and easy to read as a performance line. The plot a 2026 reader needs is: *they could have sold; they didn’t.*

### Fees — `/firm/fees` (was `/pricing`)

**Original**

> Our Fee-Based Pricing Keeps Our Goals Aligned With Yours.
>
> Our fee is transparent and is a percentage based on assets under management. … As a Registered Investment Advisor held to a fiduciary standard, we are required by law to put our clients’ interests first.

The schedule lived in the ADV, not on the page.

**New**

> A hidden fee is a fee with something to hide.
>
> Ours aren’t. The advisory fee we charge is clear, simple, and disclosed right here.

Tiers on the page. Estimator labeled **illustrative, not a quote.** ADV governs. Same schedule. Trust is not a paragraph about transparency. It is being able to drag a slider to $8M and see the math. Hiding it in a PDF was the problem. Cleanest page on the site for a reviewer.

### Wealth management — `/work/wealth-management`

Was three pages. Now one.

**Original**

> We Do More Than Manage Your Assets – We Help You Achieve Your Financial and Life Goals.
>
> At Fairview Capital, we believe that comprehensive wealth management is about our clients and their loved ones.

Real work (coordination, family, horizon), split across tabs and said like a capabilities deck.

**New**

> Wealth used to mean well-being. Somewhere along the way, Wall Street redefined it to mean casino.
>
> Managing wealth isn’t a series of bets and fees. It’s your entire financial life—your business, your taxes, your family, and your philanthropic legacy—all pulling in the same direction.

Same work, as scenes, on one page: plan first, taxes on the trade, research here, other advisors in the room, assets you don’t bill still in the picture. Three tabs made them hunt. One page lets them see the whole engagement — and recognize their life in it. “Casino” is a critique of an industry habit, not a promise about returns. Outside-assets-at-no-extra-charge should match how the firm actually bills.

### Investment management — `/work/investment-management`

Was three pages — `/investment-research-process`, `/investment-research-process/investment-philosophy`, `/investment-research-process/risk-management`. (`/investment-management` 404s.) Now one.

**Original** (philosophy + process + risk)

> Fairview’s values of disciplined decision-making, deep knowledge, and rigorous in-house investment research inform our decisions. … optimize client outcomes.
>
> … concentrated – yet adequately diversified – portfolio consisting of 15 to 25 securities…

The ideas are Andy’s. The dress is “values of disciplined decision-making.”

**New**

> A ticker is just a string of letters. We buy what’s underneath it.
>
> 25 to 30 deeply researched companies—built on five-year financial models, direct due diligence, and simple arithmetic, not short-term market guesses.

Objective (not a record), four named steps, macro admitted as inexact, themes as a search, close: *Several futures. One careful portfolio.* 15–25 became 25–30 to match how the firm talks now — confirm. No backtested chart. No “our returns.” Three pages hid the seriousness in process language. One page gives a family a line they can repeat at dinner: we buy what’s underneath the ticker.

### Team — `/team`

**Original.** Five sentences about “the benefit of our entire team” and “best possible experience.” Then bios.

**New**

> Smart and honest.
>
> Intellect means little without integrity. That is the standard for everyone here.

Then people. Nobody hires a paragraph about a team. They hire a person they would take a Sunday call from.

### Community — `/firm/community` (new)

The old site had “community” as a constituency and a few bio lines. No page whose job was “we live here.”

> Neighbors first.
>
> We don’t just write checks in Marin. We live here.

Brian coaches soccer. Peter coaches lacrosse. Andy raises puppies. Annie plays Saturday goalie. *We don’t do it for public relations.* Named, slightly unprofessional details are the only kind that read as true. Not a testimonial. Not a claim that giving improves returns.

### Technology — `/firm/technology` (new)

> Tools change. Relationships last.
>
> No machine can understand your values, weigh your trade-offs, or guide your family through life’s most personal decisions.

A 2026 family will ask about AI anyway. This answers it — judgment stays human — without becoming a product page. The site does not claim AI produces better returns.

### Answers — `/answers` (new)

Straight answers on fees, custody, strategy, operations. No dodge. A prospect comparing firms at 11 p.m. can judge the firm before the meeting. Good for compliance if every answer matches the ADV.

### Contact — `/contact`

> Let’s talk.
>
> Tell us where you are. We’ll tell you if we can help — and if we can’t, we’ll say so.

A closer would never write that last clause. Andy would. That is the filter, in one breath. Same offices, same phones, same email. Marin is labeled Greenbrae.

## 4. SEO, Local Search & Content Strategy

### What Learn is now — `/learn` (was `/our-firm/insights`)

> What we think.
>
> Not content. Not noise. Just thoughtful reporting on wealth, markets, and life.

| Channel | Line |
| :--- | :--- |
| Quarterly Letters | We write what we saw. Not what sells. |
| Investment Insights | We buy what’s underneath — research, risk, and the work we show. |
| Planning | Cash flow, tax, family, and legacy — the arithmetic you actually live. |

A marketing blog became a publishing desk. They can judge the mind before they sit down.

### Why the old Insights posts came off

Those posts were paid for so Fairview would list better in Marin. The recommendation is still to leave them off.

They were not the firm’s thinking. *Why Young Professionals Need A Financial Advisor In Marin County* and *Affluent Families In The San Francisco Bay Area Rely On Fairview Capital* are what a vendor writes when the brief is “rank in Marin.” A family who has Andy’s quarterly letter can tell in one paragraph. The typical door is about $2 million. Those titles are search phrases, not clients. They also made Fairview sound like every other RIA in the county, and “affluent families rely on us” reads like a testimonial you did not collect.

The vendor did the job they were hired for. The problem is the brief. The spend bought a ticket into a crowded list — shops that do not do their own research and will take a $250k account. A $2 million door, built on referrals, does not fail because it is hard to find for “financial advisor Marin.” It fails if the right family lands on the site and cannot tell Fairview from the others.

The money is a sunk cost. What it *did* prove is that place matters. Place stays as a fact: Greenbrae, Pittsburgh, Marin, real addresses and phones. If a piece from the old blog was actually the firm’s, it belongs in Letters, Insights, or Planning. What came off is the inventory written to rank.

### Two search jobs

They are not the same, and they do not need two voices.

| Job | What someone types or asks | What “winning” looks like |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Brand** | *Fairview Capital*, *Andy Mathieson advisor* | This site, the Profile, the ADV — not a namesake fund |
| **Category** | *RIA Marin*, *wealth manager Marin County* | In the map pack, on the first organic screen, named in the AI summary as Fairview |

The paid posts targeted the first kind of query and hoped the second would follow. It rarely does. Search rewards a clear **entity** — who you are, where you are, what kind of firm.

**On the page:** truisms. No “affluent families in Marin.”  
**In the chrome** (title, description, Google Business, schema): independent RIA; Greenbrae, Marin County; Pittsburgh; 1995; employee-owned; we do the research here. Andy would say that on the phone. He would not say it as an H1.

### What is on the site now

Root title: *Fairview Capital — independent RIA in Greenbrae, Marin County.* Description names Greenbrae, Pittsburgh, 1995, one fee. Why Fairview and Contact titles name the job and the place. Same lift-able paragraph on both: *Fairview Capital is an independent, employee-owned registered investment advisor in Greenbrae, Marin County, with a second office in Pittsburgh. Founded in 1995. We research companies in-house. We do not hire salespeople.* Schema covers the organization and both offices. H1s did not change. Do not add *What is the best RIA in Marin?* to Answers.

### What is still Google’s job

Greenbrae already has a listing (Drakes Landing, phone, hours, website). Category is *Investment company*, not investment advisor / financial planner. One generic 5-star. Photos and claim status were not confirmed. Pittsburgh does not look done — MapQuest has Brilliant Ave; LinkedIn still has Alpha Drive.

Claim and check Marin, fix the category, add real photos, add Pittsburgh once Brilliant Ave is confirmed. Without the pin, title tags will not save the listing. Do not farm reviews. If there is local budget: Dominican, the lecture series, Marin FC, Guide Dogs — not another article.

### Real letters vs listicles

| | Real Learn pieces | Old Marin listicles |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Fairview Capital* | Helps. A body of work attaches to the name. | Dilutes the name into “generic Marin advisor.” |
| AI (“what does Fairview think…”) | Helps a lot. Dated, quotable, sounds like a person. | Almost useless. The model already knows that boilerplate. |
| *RIA Marin* map pack | Almost none. Pins come from the Profile. | Almost none — and they cost more. |

Four letters a year plus a few notes the firm already believes is enough. Skip a quarter rather than invent a Marin hook. Do not buy another year of listicles, put *affluent* or *best RIA* in a title, or write for robots in Andy’s mouth.

It is working when *Fairview Capital* returns this site, this Profile, this ADV — and when AI says “independent RIA in Greenbrae, employee-owned, 1995, in-house research,” not “holistic wealth management for affluent families.”

## 6. Action Items & Next Steps

If home and Why Fairview land, the rest is the same music: the work is visible, the fee is public, the no is kind, and a family can tell this shop from the other tabs. If the adjectives want to come back, that is the old site.

Three items that cannot be finished from the site alone:

1. **Confirm the facts file for counsel** — especially 25–30 names, outside-asset billing, headcount.
2. **Google Business Profile** — claim Marin, fix the category, add photos, add Pittsburgh.
3. **Keep Learn as the work.** The old posts stay off.

That packet is easier to defend than the brochure that made Fairview sound like everyone else.
